Firm VAT dynamic modelling

1. Firm profit maximization problem

$$\max_{y_i} (1-\tau(y_i))y_i-c_i(y_i)$$
$$[y_i]: (1-\tau(y_i))- y_i \cdot \frac{\partial\tau(y_i)}{\partial y_i}-\frac{\partial c_i(y_i)}{\partial y_i}=0$$
$$\tau_{gov}(y_i)=f \text{ is known.}$$

2. Deriving marginal cost from data

From the production function with Cobb-Douglas technology:

$$Y_i = A_i X_i^{\alpha}$$

Inverting to get cost function:

$$X_i = \left(\frac{Y_i}{A_i}\right)^{1/\alpha}$$

Therefore, the cost function is:

$$c_i(y_i) = \left(\frac{y_i}{A_i}\right)^{1/\alpha}$$

Taking the derivative with respect to output:

$$\frac{\partial c_i(y_i)}{\partial y_i} = \frac{1}{\alpha} \cdot \frac{1}{A_i^{1/\alpha}} \cdot y_i^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}$$

Substituting back the cost function:

$$\frac{\partial c_i(y_i)}{\partial y_i} = \frac{1}{\alpha} \cdot \frac{c_i(y_i)}{y_i}$$

Since you observe $X_i$ (input costs) and $Y_i$ (turnover) for each firm:

$$\frac{\partial c_i(y_i)}{\partial y_i} = \frac{1}{\alpha} \cdot \frac{X_i}{Y_i}$$

Where:

  • $\alpha = 0.9864$ (your calibrated parameter)
  • $X_i$ = annual_input_k × 1000 (from data)
  • $Y_i$ = annual_turnover_k × 1000 (from data)

Therefore, for each firm:

$$\frac{\partial c_i(y_i)}{\partial y_i} = \frac{1}{0.9864} \cdot \frac{\text{input}_i}{\text{turnover}_i} = 1.014 \cdot \frac{\text{input}_i}{\text{turnover}_i}$$

3. Smooth tax function: sigmoid approximation

Instead of a step function at threshold $T^*$, we use a smooth sigmoid transition:

$$\tau(y_i) = \tau_{max} \cdot \frac{1}{1 + e^{-k(y_i - T^*)}}$$

Where:

  • $\tau_{max}$ = maximum effective VAT rate (from data)
  • $T^* = £90,000$ (VAT threshold)
  • $k$ = steepness parameter (larger $k$ → sharper transition)

The derivative of the sigmoid tax function:

$$\frac{\partial \tau(y_i)}{\partial y_i} = \tau_{max} \cdot \frac{k \cdot e^{-k(y_i - T^*)}}{(1 + e^{-k(y_i - T^*)})^2}$$

Simplifying using $\tau(y_i)$:

$$\frac{\partial \tau(y_i)}{\partial y_i} = k \cdot \tau(y_i) \cdot (1 - \frac{\tau(y_i)}{\tau_{max}})$$

4. Deriving the first-order condition

Starting from the profit maximization problem:

$$\max_{y_i} \pi_i = (1-\tau(y_i))y_i - c_i(y_i)$$

Taking the derivative with respect to $y_i$:

$$\frac{\partial \pi_i}{\partial y_i} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y_i}[(1-\tau(y_i))y_i] - \frac{\partial c_i(y_i)}{\partial y_i}$$

Applying the product rule to the first term:

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial y_i}[(1-\tau(y_i))y_i] = (1-\tau(y_i)) \cdot 1 + y_i \cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial y_i}[1-\tau(y_i)]$$
$$= (1-\tau(y_i)) - y_i \cdot \frac{\partial\tau(y_i)}{\partial y_i}$$

Setting FOC equal to zero:

$$\frac{\partial \pi_i}{\partial y_i} = (1-\tau(y_i)) - y_i \cdot \frac{\partial\tau(y_i)}{\partial y_i} - \frac{\partial c_i(y_i)}{\partial y_i} = 0$$

Rearranging to isolate $(1-\tau(y_i^*))$:

$$(1-\tau(y_i^*)) = \frac{\partial c_i(y_i^*)}{\partial y_i} + y_i^* \cdot \frac{\partial\tau(y_i^*)}{\partial y_i}$$

The correct FOC shows that marginal revenue $(1-\tau)$ equals marginal cost plus the tax wedge effect $y_i \cdot \frac{\partial\tau}{\partial y_i}$, which represents the additional tax burden from producing one more unit.

With sigmoid tax and Cobb-Douglas cost:

$$1 - \frac{\tau_{max}}{1 + e^{-k(y_i^* - T^*)}} = \frac{1}{\alpha} \cdot \frac{1}{A_i^{1/\alpha}} \cdot (y_i^*)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}} + \frac{k \cdot \tau_{max} \cdot e^{-k(y_i^* - T^*)}}{(1 + e^{-k(y_i^* - T^*)})^2}$$

5. Add uncertainty

To separate the effects of optimisation uncertainty from strategic bunching behaviour, we implement a baseline model that incorporates uncertainty into the first-order condition (FOC) optimal solutions:

5.1 Mathematical formulation

Threshold-aware uncertainty model:

$$y_{uncertainty} = y_{FOC}^* \times (1 + \varepsilon_i)$$

The uncertainty term $\varepsilon_i$ is threshold-aware and varies by firm location:

$$\varepsilon_i = \begin{cases} N(\mu_{skew}, \sigma^2) & \text{if } 0 < y_{FOC}^* - T^* < 10000 \\ N(0, \sigma^2) & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

For firms close above the threshold, the mean is asymmetric:

$$\mu_{skew} = -1.5\sigma \left(1 - \frac{y_{FOC}^* - T^*}{10000}\right)$$

Economic rationale: Firms operating close above thresholds face asymmetric risk. Negative revenue shocks keep them below the threshold (avoiding VAT registration), while positive shocks trigger VAT obligations on their entire turnover. This asymmetry creates natural precautionary behaviour through uncertainty effects alone.

5.2 Implementation

$$\text{For each firm } i: \quad y_i^{\text{uncertainty}} = y_{FOC}^* \times (1 + \varepsilon_i)$$

The uncertainty term $\varepsilon_i$ is drawn from $N(0, 0.04^2)$ with a deterministic seed based on firm characteristics to ensure reproducible results across model runs.

6. No-VAT counterfactual scenario

Building on the methodology from Liu & Lockwood (2015), we implement a counterfactual estimation approach that uses polynomial regression to construct the "no-bunching" distribution - what the firm turnover distribution would look like in the absence of VAT registration thresholds.

6.1 Theoretical foundation

The counterfactual distribution represents the structural firm turnover distribution absent tax-induced distortions. Following the bunching literature (Saez, 2010; Chetty et al., 2011; Kleven & Waseem, 2013), we estimate this by fitting a smooth polynomial to observed data while excluding the "manipulation region" around the VAT threshold.

$$f^{cf}(T) = \text{Polynomial fit excluding } [T^* - W, T^* + W]$$

where $f^{cf}(T)$ is the counterfactual density, $T^*$ is the VAT threshold, and $W$ is the exclusion window width.

6.2 Implementation methodology

Step 1: Exclusion window design

We exclude a fixed range around the threshold to remove tax-distorted observations:

$$\text{Exclusion window: } [T^* - 15\text{k}, T^* + 15\text{k}] \text{ around } T^* = £90\text{k}$$

Step 2: Distance-based feature engineering

Following Liu & Lockwood (2015), we use distance from threshold as the regression feature:

$$y_{dist} = T - T^* \text{ (distance from £90k threshold)}$$

Step 3: Third-order polynomial regression

The counterfactual density is estimated using a cubic polynomial:

$$f^{cf}(T) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 y_{dist} + \beta_2 y_{dist}^2 + \beta_3 y_{dist}^3$$

fitted to observed data outside the exclusion window using ordinary least squares.

7. Extracting elasticity

We extract firm-level elasticities by comparing two key distributions:

  • Real-world distribution: Observed firm turnover under current VAT system
  • No-VAT distribution: Polynomial-fitted distribution representing firm behaviour without VAT distortions

Our FOC + uncertainty model is calibrated to bridge these two distributions, allowing us to derive the distribution of elasticities across firms.

7.1 Firm-level elasticity distribution

Individual firm elasticity:

For each firm $i$, the elasticity is derived from the ratio of responses:

$$\varepsilon_i = \frac{\ln(y_i^{\text{no VAT}}) - \ln(y_i^{\text{observed}})}{\ln(1-\tau) - \ln(1)}$$

where $\tau$ represents the firm's effective VAT burden.

7.2 Aggregate elasticity measures

Mean elasticity:

$$\bar{\varepsilon} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \varepsilon_i$$

Weighted average elasticity:

$$\varepsilon_{\text{weighted}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \varepsilon_i y_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i y_i}$$

where $w_i$ are firm sampling weights.
